Things to do in Uzbekistan

The beating heart of the ancient Silk Road, turquoise-domed madrasas rising from desert cities, and a culture of hospitality shaped by centuries as a crossroads of civilizations.

Travel smart

Bottled water recommended. Not customary — rounding up is fine.

Samarkand, Bukhara, and Khiva together formed some of the most important cities on the entire Silk Road, and their turquoise-tiled madrasas, minarets, and mausoleums remain among the best-preserved examples of Islamic architecture anywhere in the world. Uzbekistan is one of only two doubly landlocked countries on Earth (surrounded entirely by other landlocked countries), a geographic quirk shared only with Liechtenstein, despite being vastly larger in both size and population.

When to go

SpringApril–May

Mild and blooming, widely considered the best season nationally, with comfortable temperatures for exploring the Silk Road cities.

SummerJune–August

Very hot, especially in desert areas — early morning sightseeing is essential during this stretch.

AutumnSeptember–October

Comfortable temperatures and harvest season, considered alongside spring as the best window to visit.

WinterNovember–March

Cold, particularly at night, with a much quieter tourism season nationwide.

Food & drink

4

Eat plov, Uzbekistan's national rice dish

A rice dish cooked with meat, carrots, and onions in a large communal cauldron (kazan), with regional variations found across the country.

foodeveningYear-round

Try Uzbek non bread from a traditional tandoor oven

Round, stamped flatbread baked in clay ovens, central to Uzbek daily life and treated with genuine cultural reverence — it's considered bad luck to place it upside down.

foodmorningYear-round

Try Uzbek shashlik and samsa

Grilled skewered meat and baked pastry parcels, both genuine street food staples reflecting Uzbekistan's Silk Road-era culinary crossroads.

foodeveningYear-round

Attend a traditional Uzbek tea ceremony

Green tea served in small bowls is central to Uzbek hospitality, typically accompanied by dried fruit, nuts, and sweets in a genuine daily social ritual.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for Uzbekistan?

As of January 2026, US, UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian passport holders can enter visa-free for up to 30 days, a significant expansion of the country's earlier, more restrictive visa policy.

▾Do I need to register my accommodation?

Yes — every foreign visitor must register their accommodation within three days of arrival, a requirement that applies under both visa-free and visa entry; hotels typically handle this automatically.

▾How many days does the Silk Road route need?

9-11 days lets you comfortably combine Tashkent, Samarkand, Bukhara, and Khiva, given the travel distances between these historic cities across the country.

▾Is Uzbekistan safe and easy to travel around?

Yes, generally considered one of the more stable and tourist-friendly countries in Central Asia, with a well-developed high-speed rail network connecting the main Silk Road cities.

▾Is Uzbekistan affordable?

Yes, among the more budget-friendly destinations in Asia, with strong value for food, accommodation, and the high-speed rail network connecting its historic cities.

▾How much does it cost to visit Samarkand's Registan?

Foreign visitor entry to the Registan complex runs a modest UZS 50,000-60,000 (about $4-5). Many of Uzbekistan's Silk Road monuments carry similarly affordable entry fees relative to their scale and significance.
