Things to do in Vatican City

The smallest sovereign state in the world, the seat of the Roman Catholic Church, and one of the highest concentrations of Renaissance art anywhere on Earth packed into under half a square kilometre.

Vatican City is the smallest internationally recognized sovereign state in the world by both area and population, at roughly 0.49 square kilometres, yet it houses the Sistine Chapel's ceiling by Michelangelo and St. Peter's Basilica, the largest church in the world by interior area. As the seat of the Roman Catholic Church and residence of the Pope, it holds a unique dual status as both a sovereign state and the spiritual center for well over a billion Catholics worldwide.

When to go

SpringApril–May

Mild and pleasant, though a popular window that coincides with Easter crowds and major religious observances.

SummerJune–August

Hot and the busiest tourist season, with the longest queues for the Vatican Museums and St. Peter's Basilica.

AutumnSeptember–October

Comfortable temperatures and somewhat thinner crowds than summer, a strong overall window.

WinterNovember–March

Cool but generally mild by northern European standards, with the shortest queues outside the Christmas and New Year period.

Common questions

▾Do I need a separate visa for Vatican City?

No — there's no separate visa, since entry is via Italy, so US, UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian passport holders follow standard Schengen visa-free terms for Italy.

▾How much time do I need for the Vatican Museums and Sistine Chapel?

A minimum of 3-4 hours is recommended to see the Vatican Museums' highlights and the Sistine Chapel without excessive rushing, and booking timed-entry tickets in advance is strongly advised given the crowds.

▾Can I climb to the top of St. Peter's dome?

Yes, for an additional fee, with an option to walk the entire way or take an elevator partway before a final steep climb — the view over Rome and St. Peter's Square is widely considered worth the effort.

▾What should I wear to visit St. Peter's Basilica?

Modest dress covering shoulders and knees is strictly enforced for both men and women, with visitors turned away at the door if not appropriately dressed.

▾Is Vatican City really its own country?

Yes — it's the smallest internationally recognized sovereign state in the world, with its own postal system, currency issuance rights, and a small Swiss Guard defense force, despite being entirely surrounded by Rome.
