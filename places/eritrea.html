Things to do in Eritrea

A capital city so architecturally intact from the Italian colonial era that it earned UNESCO recognition as a living Art Deco museum, a Red Sea coastline still genuinely undiscovered, and one of the most isolated but visually striking destinations in Africa.

Travel smart

Bottled or purified water only. 10% at restaurants; guides and drivers appreciate a daily tip.

Asmara, Eritrea's capital, is a UNESCO World Heritage site recognized as one of the best-preserved collections of Italian Art Deco and modernist architecture anywhere in the world, a genuinely surreal urban landscape frozen largely as it was built during the 1930s Italian colonial period. Eritrea's Dahlak Archipelago, a chain of over 200 islands in the Red Sea, remains one of the least-dived and least-visited reef systems in the entire region, a product of the country's decades of relative isolation rather than any lack of natural significance.

When to go

Cool season (highlands)October–March

The most comfortable window for Asmara and the highlands, with mild temperatures at altitude.

Hot season (coast)October–March

The best window for the Red Sea coast and Dahlak Archipelago, avoiding the extreme summer heat of the Eritrean lowlands.

Highland wet seasonJune–September

The main rainy period in the highlands around Asmara, with the coast remaining largely dry and extremely hot.

Extreme coastal heatApril–September

The Red Sea coast and lowlands become extremely hot during this period, among the hottest inhabited regions on Earth.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for Eritrea?

All nationalities, including US, UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian citizens, need a visa arranged in advance through an Eritrean embassy, with additional travel permits typically required for movement outside Asmara.

▾Why is Asmara so architecturally significant?

It's a UNESCO World Heritage site recognized as one of the best-preserved collections of Italian Art Deco and modernist architecture anywhere in the world, largely frozen as it was built during the 1930s Italian colonial period.

▾Is independent travel possible in Eritrea?

Movement is significantly restricted, with permits typically required to travel outside Asmara, making organized, permit-arranged itineraries the practical default rather than a preference.

▾How many days does Eritrea need?

7-9 days lets you combine Asmara's architecture with a permitted excursion to the Red Sea coast or Dahlak Archipelago, given the additional permit logistics involved in traveling beyond the capital.

▾Is internet access available for tourists in Eritrea?

Access is limited and heavily restricted nationwide, so it's worth planning for significantly reduced connectivity throughout your visit.

▾Is it easy to visit Eritrea as a tourist?

Eritrea remains one of the more tightly controlled countries for independent travel — most regions outside Asmara require a travel permit arranged in advance, and foreign journalists and independent researchers face particular restrictions. Asmara's Italian-era Art Deco architecture is a genuine highlight for visitors who do make it, but check your government's current travel advisory and permit requirements closely before planning a trip.
