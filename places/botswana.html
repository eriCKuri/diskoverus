Things to do in Botswana

The Okavango Delta flooding the Kalahari from the inside out, and some of Africa's most exclusive, low-impact safari camps.

Travel smart

Bottled water recommended outside major cities. 10% at restaurants; guides and drivers appreciate a daily tip.

Botswana pioneered a 'low-volume, high-value' tourism model that strictly limits visitor numbers, keeping its parks uncrowded and its wildlife densities among the highest in Africa. The Okavango Delta is one of the few major rivers on Earth that never reaches the sea — it fans out into the Kalahari Desert instead, creating a seasonal wetland from what should be dry sand.

When to go

Flood seasonMay–October

The Okavango Delta fills to its fullest as floodwater arrives from Angolan highlands, concentrating wildlife and making mokoro travel possible even where it wasn't months earlier.

Peak dry seasonJuly–October

Vegetation thins and water sources shrink, pushing wildlife toward remaining channels and waterholes — the most reliable window for sightings.

Green seasonNovember–April

Lush landscapes, newborn animals, and dramatic skies, with lower rates and thinner crowds — though some camps close and roads can flood.

Shoulder monthsApril–May, October–November

A reasonable compromise between the flood season's scenery and the dry season's concentrated wildlife, with fewer visitors than peak months.

Safari in Moremi Game Reserve

Explore the Makgadikgadi salt pans

Botswana holds one of the largest remaining populations of painted wild dogs, one of Africa's rarest and most endangered predators.

Common questions

▾Is Botswana safe to visit?

Yes — Botswana has one of Africa's most stable governments and low crime rates, and its safari industry is built around small, well-run camps. The real risks are the same as anywhere on safari: wildlife encounters, so guides' safety briefings are worth taking seriously.

▾Why is Botswana so much more expensive than Kenya or Tanzania?

Botswana deliberately limits visitor numbers to protect its ecosystems, which means fewer camps, smaller groups, and higher per-night rates rather than budget lodges. It's a genuinely different model — 'low-volume, high-value' — built for a different kind of trip than a classic East Africa safari.

▾How many days do you need in Botswana?

A week focused on the Okavango Delta and Chobe covers the highlights well. Ten days lets you add Moremi or the Makgadikgadi salt pans without feeling rushed, since flights between camps eat into shorter itineraries.

▾Do I need a visa for Botswana?

Most US, UK, and EU passport holders can enter visa-free for up to 90 days. Confirm the current requirement for your specific nationality before booking, as rules vary by country.

▾When is the best time to see wildlife in Botswana?

July through October, the peak dry season, concentrates animals around shrinking water sources for the most reliable sightings. May through October also floods the Okavango Delta, making mokoro and boat safaris possible.

▾Is the Okavango Delta only accessible by plane?

Most delta camps are reached by light aircraft from Maun, Botswana's safari hub, since roads into the delta itself are limited or nonexistent. It adds cost but is part of what keeps visitor numbers — and environmental impact — low.
