Things to do in Trinidad and Tobago

The birthplace of steelpan and Carnival's biggest Caribbean celebration outside Brazil, plus a sister island of quiet reefs and rainforest that feels a world away from the party.

Travel smart

Stick to bottled or filtered water. 10–15% at restaurants is standard; often added automatically.

Trinidad's Carnival is considered the largest and most influential in the Caribbean, and the country is the birthplace of both steelpan (the only acoustic instrument invented in the 20th century) and calypso and soca music, exporting a musical influence far beyond its small size. Tobago, its quieter sister island, holds the oldest legally protected rainforest reserve in the Western Hemisphere, designated in 1776.

When to go

Dry seasonJanuary–May

The most popular window, with lower rainfall and Trinidad's Carnival (February or March) as the year's biggest draw.

Early wet seasonJune–July

Increasing rain but still workable, with fewer crowds and lower rates.

Hurricane season (lower risk)August–November

Trinidad and Tobago sit outside the main Caribbean hurricane belt, making this a lower-risk wet season than most of the region, though rain is regular.

Transition periodDecember

Rains tapering off as the dry season and Carnival preparations begin.

Food & drink

Eat doubles, Trinidad's iconic street food

Curried chickpeas sandwiched between two pieces of fried bara bread, sold from street carts and considered a genuine national breakfast institution.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for Trinidad and Tobago?

Most Western passport holders, including US, UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian citizens, can enter visa-free for up to 90 days for tourism.

▾Is Trinidad and Tobago's Carnival worth planning a trip around?

Yes, widely considered one of the Caribbean's premier Carnival celebrations, with elaborate costumes, steelpan competitions, and street parties (mas) — book accommodation many months ahead, as dates shift each year with the Christian calendar.

▾Trinidad or Tobago — which should I prioritize?

Trinidad has the Carnival energy, birdwatching, and urban culture; Tobago is quieter, with better beaches and diving — many visitors combine both via the short inter-island flight or ferry.

▾Is Trinidad and Tobago outside the main hurricane risk zone?

Yes, its far-southern position in the Caribbean puts it at notably lower hurricane risk than most of the region, though tropical rain remains common in the wet season.

▾How's the food scene different from other Caribbean islands?

Trinidad's cuisine reflects a genuinely distinct blend of Indian, African, Chinese, and Creole influences — dishes like doubles and roti reflect the island's significant Indo-Trinidadian population, a food culture found nowhere else in the Caribbean.

▾How much does it cost to visit Trinidad's turtle-watching beaches?

Guided leatherback turtle-watching tours at beaches like Grande Riviere typically run $20-40 USD per person during nesting season (March-August), which covers the mandatory local guide required to protect the nesting sites. Access outside a guided tour usually isn't permitted during peak nesting months.
