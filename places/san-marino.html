The world's oldest surviving republic, three medieval fortress towers crowning a mountain above the Italian countryside, and a country entirely surrounded by another.

San Marino claims to be the oldest surviving sovereign state and constitutional republic in the world, founded, according to tradition, in 301 AD by a Christian stonemason fleeing persecution — making it older than the Roman Empire's fall itself. Its three medieval fortress towers, perched along a ridge of Monte Titano, have watched over the surrounding Italian countryside for nearly a thousand years and remain the country's defining visual symbol, appearing on its flag.

When to go

SpringApril–May

Mild and green, a good window for exploring the fortress towers before summer heat and crowds.

SummerJune–August

Warm and the busiest season, drawing day-trippers from the nearby Adriatic coast.

AutumnSeptember–October

Comfortable temperatures and fewer crowds, a strong window for walking the ridge trails between towers.

WinterNovember–March

Cold and occasionally snowy at altitude, with a quiet season nationally outside the Christmas market period.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for San Marino?

There's no formal border control between San Marino and Italy, so entry follows standard Schengen visa-free terms for eligible passport holders — up to 90 days in any 180-day period.

▾How do I get to San Marino?

There's no airport or train station in San Marino itself — most visitors arrive by bus or car from Rimini on the Italian Adriatic coast, about 45 minutes away.

▾Is San Marino worth a day trip from Italy?

Yes for most visitors — the three fortress towers, medieval old town, and sweeping views over the surrounding Italian countryside make for a rewarding half-day to full-day excursion.

▾Is San Marino really the world's oldest republic?

It claims that title, tracing its founding to 301 AD, though the claim rests partly on tradition — regardless, it's among the smallest and most enduring sovereign states in the world.

▾Can I get a San Marino passport stamp as a souvenir?

Yes, a novelty stamp is available for a small fee at the tourist office, popular with visitors even though it holds no official immigration function.
