Things to do in Ireland

Cliffs that drop straight into the Atlantic, a pub culture built for conversation and live music, and a small enough island that you can circle most of it in a single well-planned road trip.

Ireland's literary output per capita is remarkable for a country its size — four Nobel laureates in literature, from Yeats to Beckett to Heaney — and its oral storytelling tradition still shows up nightly in pub sessions across the country. The Wild Atlantic Way traces nearly 2,600 kilometres of coastline, one of the longest defined coastal touring routes in the world.

When to go

SpringMarch–May

Green and blooming with fewer crowds, though rain remains likely any given day.

SummerJune–August

The warmest, driest, and busiest months, with long evenings good for late coastal drives.

AutumnSeptember–October

Fewer tourists, mellow light for photography, and prices easing after summer.

WinterNovember–February

Short, wet, and windy — but cozy pub culture and dramatic storm-watching along the west coast.

Adventure & outdoors

4

Drive the Ring of Kerry

A roughly 179-kilometre loop through Killarney National Park, coastal villages, and mountain passes — drive counterclockwise to avoid tour bus traffic.

adventurefullApril–October

Hike the Dingle Peninsula's coastal trails

A quieter, wilder alternative to the Ring of Kerry, with beehive huts, ocean views, and a genuinely Irish-speaking (Gaeltacht) region.

adventurefullApril–October

Surf at Lahinch or Bundoran on the Wild Atlantic Way

Ireland's west coast has a genuine, growing surf scene, with reliable Atlantic swell and surf schools for beginners in both towns.

adventurefullYear-round, best autumn swell

Cycle the Great Western Greenway in County Mayo

A 42-kilometre traffic-free cycling trail along a former railway line, passing coastline, bogland, and small villages.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for Ireland?

US, UK, Canadian, and Australian passport holders can enter visa-free for up to 90 days. Ireland sits outside the Schengen Area, so it has its own separate entry rules rather than the EU's shared system.

▾Is renting a car necessary to see the coast?

For the Wild Atlantic Way and rural areas, yes — public transport thins out fast outside Dublin and Cork, and driving is the only practical way to reach places like the Cliffs of Moher at your own pace or the Ring of Kerry.

▾How's the weather really — is it always raining?

Rain is frequent and can happen in any season, but it's typically light and passing rather than constant downpours. Layer up and don't let forecast rain deter outdoor plans; the weather shifts quickly.

▾Is Irish pub culture as central as people say?

Yes — pubs function as community hubs for conversation, live traditional music sessions, and storytelling, not just drinking. A good session pub in Galway or Doolin is one of the best ways to experience Irish culture directly.

▾How many days should I plan for Dublin versus the countryside?

2-3 days covers Dublin's core sights well; most of what makes Ireland distinctive — the coastline, small towns, and rural pubs — is outside the capital, so budget the majority of a trip for a road trip loop.

▾How much does it cost to visit the Cliffs of Moher?

The visitor center and cliff-top walkway charge around €10-12 per adult, one of the more modest entrance fees for a headline natural attraction anywhere in Europe. Parking is included in the ticket price, which isn't always the case at similar sites elsewhere.
