Two oceans, a wine valley, and some of the best wildlife viewing on the continent.

Travel smart

Bottled water recommended outside major cities. 10% at restaurants; guides and drivers appreciate a daily tip.

South Africa has three capital cities, splitting legislative, judicial and administrative power between Cape Town, Bloemfontein and Pretoria — a compromise from its founding. It's also one of the only places on earth where you can safari for Big Five game and surf or whale-watch on a world-class coastline within a single day's drive.

When to go

Kruger dry seasonMay–September

Thinning bush and scarce water concentrate wildlife around rivers and pans — the most reliable window for game viewing.

Cape Town summerOctober–March

Warm, dry and sunny on the Cape — peak season for beaches, hiking and the winelands, opposite Kruger's wet season.

Whale seasonJune–November

Southern right whales calve close to shore along the Western Cape, with Hermanus among the best land-based whale-watching spots anywhere.

Shoulder monthsApril & September–October

A reasonable compromise between Kruger's dry season and Cape Town's summer, with fewer crowds than either peak.

Culture & history

4

Walk through District Six and the Bo-Kaap

A former forced-removal site turned museum, and a hillside neighbourhood of brightly painted houses with deep Cape Malay roots — two sides of Cape Town's layered history.

culturehalfYear-round

Visit Robben Island

The prison island where Nelson Mandela spent eighteen of his twenty-seven years in custody, now a museum run partly by former political prisoners as guides.

culturehalfYear-round, book ahead

Visit the Apartheid Museum in Johannesburg

An unflinching, well-built museum covering the rise and fall of apartheid, widely regarded as essential context before or after a safari trip.

culturehalfYear-round

Visit an ostrich farm in the Karoo

Oudtshoorn built its economy on ostrich feathers a century ago and still farms them today — tours cover everything from eggs to an ostrich-back ride.

Common questions

▾Is South Africa safe to visit?

South Africa has a reputation for higher crime rates than other safari destinations, but the risk is concentrated in specific urban areas rather than the parks and wine country most itineraries focus on. Kruger, the Garden Route and Cape Town's main tourist areas are generally safe with normal precautions — stick to well-reviewed accommodation and avoid walking alone at night in Johannesburg.

▾How much does it cost to enter Kruger National Park?

Kruger's daily conservation fee runs on three tiers: roughly R134 per adult for South African citizens and residents, R275 for SADC nationals, and R602 for international visitors — more than four times the local rate. Bring a passport to be charged the correct tier at the gate. Rates are set for SANParks' November-to-October tariff cycle and adjust each year.

▾How many days do you need in South Africa?

10-14 days covers the classic combination well: three or four days in Kruger, a few days in Cape Town and the winelands, and a couple more along the Garden Route if time allows. A tighter one-week trip usually means choosing just Kruger and Cape Town.

▾Do I need a visa for South Africa?

US, UK and most EU passport holders can enter visa-free for up to 90 days. Check the current list before you fly, since South Africa has adjusted its visa-exemption agreements with several countries in recent years.

▾When is the best time for a Kruger safari?

The dry winter months from May through September are the classic safari window — thinning vegetation and animals clustering around water sources make sightings far more reliable. The wet summer months bring lush scenery and newborn animals, but denser bush that makes spotting wildlife harder.

▾Is South Africa expensive?

South Africa is one of the better-value safari destinations thanks to a weak rand against the dollar and euro, reading as a $$ trip overall even with Kruger's higher international entry tier factored in. Self-drive safaris through Kruger, which don't require a guide, are a genuinely affordable way to see Big Five wildlife compared to fully guided lodge safaris elsewhere in Africa.
