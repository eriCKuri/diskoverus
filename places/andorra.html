Things to do in Andorra

A tiny co-principality wedged between France and Spain, duty-free shopping alongside genuinely serious Pyrenees skiing, and one of the highest-altitude capitals in Europe.

Travel smart

Andorra is one of the world's few remaining co-principalities, jointly headed by the President of France and the Bishop of Urgell in Spain, an unusual dual-sovereignty arrangement dating back to a 13th-century feudal charter that's never been fully dissolved. Despite its tiny size, smaller than most cities, its position high in the Pyrenees gives it some of Europe's best-value skiing alongside duty-free shopping that's long made it a magnet for day-trippers from both neighboring countries.

When to go

Ski seasonDecember–April

The country's main tourism season, with reliable snow across its Pyrenees resorts and a lively après-ski scene.

Spring shoulderMay–June

Snow melting at altitude, with hiking trails opening progressively as the season warms.

SummerJuly–August

Warm valley temperatures and cool mountain air, the best window for hiking and countryside touring.

Autumn shoulderSeptember–November

Comfortable temperatures and fewer crowds before ski season begins, with vivid mountain forest color.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for Andorra?

There's no border control between Andorra and its Schengen neighbors France and Spain, so entry follows standard Schengen visa-free terms for eligible passport holders, up to 90 days in any 180-day period.

▾How do I get to Andorra?

There's no airport or train station in Andorra itself, most visitors arrive by bus or car from Barcelona (about 3 hours) or Toulouse (about 2.5 hours).

▾Is Andorra just for skiing and shopping?

Those are the main draws, but the Pyrenees setting also offers strong hiking, thermal spas, and small Romanesque churches scattered through the valleys for those staying beyond a quick trip.

▾How many days does Andorra need?

2-4 days is typical, often as an add-on to a wider Spain or France itinerary rather than a standalone destination.

▾Is Andorra expensive?

Mid-range overall, with duty-free shopping offering genuine savings on electronics, alcohol, and tobacco, though ski resort accommodation can be pricier in peak season.

▾How much does it cost to visit Caldea thermal spa?

A standard day pass to Caldea, one of Andorra's best-known attractions, runs around €45-75 depending on the day and package. It's one of the pricier standalone attractions in the country, reflecting Andorra's broader positioning as a higher-cost destination.
