Things to do in Uganda

Mountain gorilla trekking through dense rainforest, the source of the Nile, and a wildlife density that led Winston Churchill to call it the 'Pearl of Africa.'

Travel smart

Bottled or purified water only. 10% at restaurants; guides and drivers appreciate a daily tip.

Uganda holds roughly half of the world's remaining wild mountain gorilla population, tracked on foot through Bwindi Impenetrable Forest's dense rainforest, one of the most sought-after wildlife experiences on Earth. It's also where the Nile River's source (or one of them) was famously identified by 19th-century explorers, and its landscape shifts from savanna to rainforest to snow-capped mountains in the Rwenzoris within a single country.

When to go

Dry season (long)June–August

The most popular window for both gorilla trekking and general safari viewing, with drier, more comfortable trail conditions.

Wet season (short)September–November

Increasing rain but still workable for trekking, with fewer crowds and lower permit availability pressure.

Dry season (short)December–February

A second reliable dry window, popular for both gorilla trekking and wildlife safaris in Queen Elizabeth and Murchison Falls.

Wet season (long)March–May

The heaviest rains, making forest trails muddy and difficult — the lowest season for trekking, though rates often drop.

Wildlife

9

Trek mountain gorillas in Bwindi Impenetrable Forest

Uganda holds roughly half the world's remaining wild mountain gorilla population, tracked on foot through dense rainforest for a strictly limited hour with a habituated family.

naturefullJune–August, December–February

Track chimpanzees in Kibale National Park

One of the highest chimpanzee densities in Africa, with guided treks offering close encounters alongside a dozen other primate species.

naturefullYear-round, driest June–Aug

Spot tree-climbing lions in Queen Elizabeth National Park

A rare behavior found in only a few African lion populations, with lions regularly seen lounging in fig trees in the park's Ishasha sector.

Go on a game drive in Kidepo Valley National Park

One of Africa's most remote and rugged parks, on the border with South Sudan, with wildlife and landscapes rarely seen by international visitors.

Visit the Uganda Wildlife Education Centre in Entebbe

A rehabilitation-focused zoo and conservation center offering close, ethical encounters with Ugandan wildlife not easily seen elsewhere.

Birdwatch in Bwindi and Mabamba Swamp

Uganda holds one of the highest bird counts of any African country, including the prehistoric-looking shoebill stork found reliably at Mabamba.

Take a launch cruise on the Kazinga Channel

A narrow waterway connecting two lakes in Queen Elizabeth National Park, with some of the highest hippo concentrations found anywhere.

Track golden monkeys in Mgahinga Gorilla National Park

An endangered primate found only in the Virunga volcanic range, tracked on forested slopes shared with a smaller mountain gorilla population.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for Uganda?

Most nationalities, including US, UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian citizens, need an e-visa applied for online before arrival, typically approved within about a week for stays up to 90 days.

▾How much does gorilla trekking cost and is it worth it?

Permits run several hundred dollars per person and require advance booking, often months ahead in peak season — most visitors who've done it describe the hour spent with a habituated gorilla family as genuinely unforgettable and worth the cost.

▾How physically demanding is gorilla trekking?

It varies significantly by which gorilla family you're assigned and where they've moved that day — treks can range from a couple of hours to a full day of steep, muddy forest hiking, so reasonable fitness is recommended.

▾Is Uganda safe for tourists?

Established tourist circuits (Bwindi, Queen Elizabeth, Murchison Falls, Kampala) see regular safe tourism; as with any destination, check current advisories for border regions before finalizing an itinerary.

▾Can I combine gorilla trekking with a broader safari?

Yes, and most visitors do — Uganda pairs Bwindi's gorillas with Queen Elizabeth National Park's tree-climbing lions or Murchison Falls' game drives and boat safaris for a fuller trip.
