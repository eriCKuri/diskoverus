Travel smart

Tap water is safe to drink. Not expected — rounding up is enough.

Norway's coastline, fjords included, is long enough to circle the earth roughly one and a half times, and almost none of it is straight. Daylight is the country's real variable — near-endless sun in June, four or five hours of it in December — and what's worth doing changes completely depending on which one you get.

When to go

Midnight sunJune–August

Long light, open mountain roads, sunny fjord cruises and the warmest hiking weather. Also the busiest and priciest months.

Shoulder seasonMay & September

May brings waterfalls at full snowmelt power and green valleys before the crowds; September starts to bring the dark back for aurora hunting.

Northern lights windowSeptember–March

Needs real darkness, so this only works once the midnight sun ends. Tromsø, Alta and the Lofoten Islands are the reliable bases.

Deep winterDecember–February

Snowshoeing, dog sledding, whale watching off the north coast and the best statistical odds for aurora, alongside short days and closed mountain passes.

Adventure & outdoors

5

Hike to Preikestolen

A flat rock plateau 604 metres above Lysefjord — a demanding but non-technical hike that ends at one of the most photographed views in the country.

adventurefullApr–Oct, snow-free trail

Hike Trolltunga

A longer, tougher day than Preikestolen — a rock ledge jutting horizontally over a lake, ten to twelve hours round trip.

adventurefullJun–Sep

Go dog sledding

Run by mushers across Finnmark and around Tromsø, usually paired with a night in a lavvo tent or cabin under whatever the sky is doing.

adventurefullDec–Apr

Ski or snowboard in the fjords

Norway's downhill scene doesn't get the attention of the Alps, but resorts like Hemsedal and Trysil, and heli-skiing around the Lyngen Alps, deliver serious terrain.

adventurefullDec–Apr

Stand at the North Cape

A cliff plateau marketed as continental Europe's northernmost point, with the midnight sun visible from mid-May to the end of July.

adventurehalfMay–Jul for midnight sun

Coast & water

6

Cruise or drive the Geirangerfjord

A UNESCO-listed fjord with waterfalls dropping straight into the water and farms clinging to ledges that look impossible to reach.

naturefullMay–September

Ride the Flåm Railway

One of the steepest standard-gauge railways in the world, twenty minutes down a mountainside past waterfalls, connecting to Sognefjord below.

adventurehalfYear-round

Explore the Lofoten Islands

Jagged granite peaks rising straight from the sea, red fishing cabins called rorbuer, and white-sand beaches that look tropical until you touch the water.

naturemultiJun–Aug for hiking, Sep–Mar for aurora

Ride the Norway in a Nutshell route

A single-day chain of train, fjord ferry and mountain bus connecting Bergen and Oslo through Flåm and the Aurlandsfjord.

adventurefullYear-round, best views May–Sep

Kayak a fjord at your own pace

Rentals are available on most of the accessible fjords — Nærøyfjord and Geirangerfjord both have calm enough water for a half-day paddle.

adventurehalfMay–September

Take the Hurtigruten coastal ferry

A working mail-and-passenger route running the length of the coast from Bergen to Kirkenes, stopping in dozens of small ports along the way.

adventuremultiYear-round, different scenery each season

Common questions

▾Is Norway safe to visit?

Yes — Norway is one of the safest countries in the world for tourists, with very low crime rates. The main risks travelers actually encounter relate to weather and terrain on hikes and fjord excursions rather than crime, so proper gear and checking conditions matter more than typical safety precautions.

▾How much do Norway's fjord cruises and hikes cost?

Many of Norway's most famous views, like the hike to Pulpit Rock (Preikestolen), are free — the cost is in getting there and, often, a paid ferry or bus connection. Fjord cruises and boat tours vary widely by length and route, typically running anywhere from roughly $50 for a short scenic ferry to $150-plus for a multi-hour fjord cruise.

▾How many days do you need in Norway?

10-12 days covers a fjord-focused route well — Bergen, the Sognefjord or Geirangerfjord region, and time in Oslo. A tighter one-week trip usually means picking one fjord region rather than covering the full west coast.

▾Do I need a visa for Norway?

US, UK, Canadian and most other Western passport holders can visit visa-free for up to 90 days within the Schengen Area, which Norway is part of despite not being an EU member. Longer stays or nationalities outside the visa-waiver list need a Schengen visa arranged in advance.

▾What's the best time to visit Norway?

June through August offers the longest daylight hours (the midnight sun in the north) and the most reliable weather for fjord hikes and cruises. Winter (December-February) is the season for the northern lights but comes with short days and much colder conditions.

▾Is Norway expensive?

Norway is one of the most expensive countries in Europe, if not the world, for dining out and alcohol in particular. Self-catering and camping, both well-supported by Norway's right-to-roam access laws, are the main ways budget travelers keep costs down.
