Things to do in Comoros

An Indian Ocean archipelago nicknamed the 'Perfume Islands' for its production of the world's most sought-after ylang-ylang, an active volcano holding one of the largest craters on Earth, and a genuinely under-the-radar destination.

Travel smart

Bottled or purified water only. 10% at restaurants; guides and drivers appreciate a daily tip.

Comoros produces a significant share of the world's ylang-ylang, an essential oil central to the perfume industry, earning the archipelago its nickname the 'Perfume Islands' and giving it an outsized role in global luxury fragrance production relative to its tiny size. Mount Karthala, an active volcano on the main island of Grande Comore, holds one of the largest volcanic craters on Earth, and its periodic eruptions have shaped both the island's dramatic landscape and its communities' long-standing relationship with the mountain.

When to go

Dry, cool seasonMay–October

The most popular and comfortable window nationally, with lower humidity and calmer seas for island travel.

Warm, wet seasonNovember–April

Warmer and more humid with regular rain, including the Indian Ocean cyclone risk window.

Cyclone risk windowJanuary–March

The highest tropical cyclone risk period — monitor forecasts closely if traveling in this window.

Transition periodsApril, October–November

Shoulder months balancing decent weather with fewer crowds between the two main seasons.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for Comoros?

Most nationalities, including US, UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian citizens, can get a visa on arrival, typically allowing stays up to 45 days.

▾Why are the Comoros called the 'Perfume Islands'?

The archipelago produces a significant share of the world's ylang-ylang, an essential oil central to the perfume industry, giving it an outsized role in global luxury fragrance production.

▾Can I climb Mount Karthala?

Yes — the active volcano on Grande Comore holds one of the largest craters on Earth, and guided treks to the summit offer a genuinely dramatic volcanic landscape.

▾How many days does Comoros need?

7-9 days lets you combine Grande Comore's volcano and capital with a trip to Mohéli or Anjouan's quieter beaches and reefs, given inter-island travel requires small boats or flights.

▾Is Comoros affected by cyclones?

Yes, particularly January through March, the peak Indian Ocean cyclone risk window — check current forecasts if traveling during this period.

▾How much does it cost to climb Mount Karthala?

There's no formal park entrance fee, but a mandatory local guide for the active volcano trek typically costs $20-40 USD per day. Comoros sees very little organized tourism, so arranging guides and logistics in advance through a local contact is essential rather than optional.
