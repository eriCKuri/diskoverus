Things to do in El Salvador

Central America's smallest country turned surprise surf destination, a chain of volcanoes visible from nearly everywhere, and a security transformation that's reshaped its tourism reputation.

El Salvador has undergone one of the most dramatic security transformations of any country in recent years, moving from a reputation defined by gang violence to being marketed as one of the safer destinations in Central America, a shift that has fueled a rapid rise in surf and adventure tourism. Its 'Ruta de las Flores' and volcanic highland towns sit within view of over 20 volcanoes packed into a country roughly the size of Massachusetts, giving it one of the highest volcano densities anywhere on Earth.

When to go

Dry seasonNovember–April

The most popular window, with clear skies and the best conditions for both surf breaks and volcano hiking.

Early wet seasonMay–July

Regular but often predictable afternoon rain, with lush green highlands and fewer crowds.

Peak wet seasonAugust–October

The heaviest rains, though coastal surf breaks often still work well between storms.

Transition periodOctober–November

Rains easing as the dry season approaches, a reasonable value window.

Adventure & outdoors

5

Surf the point breaks of El Tunco and El Zonte

Consistent, high-quality Pacific breaks that have made El Salvador's coast a fast-rising international surf destination.

adventurefullMarch–October, best swells

Hike Santa Ana Volcano to its turquoise crater lake

El Salvador's tallest volcano, with a moderate guided hike rewarded by a sulfur-tinted turquoise crater lake at the summit.

adventurefullNovember–April, driest

Hike Izalco Volcano, once known as the 'Lighthouse of the Pacific'

A near-perfect cone volcano that was active and visible from ships far offshore until the mid-20th century, now dormant and hikeable.

adventurefullNovember–April

Hike El Boquerón crater near San Salvador

A dormant volcano crater within easy reach of the capital, offering rim trails and views into a smaller secondary cone inside.

adventurehalfYear-round

Surf or swim at Punta Roca in La Libertad

Considered one of the best right-hand point breaks in Central America, drawing serious surfers to the fishing town of La Libertad.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for El Salvador?

Most Western passport holders, including US, UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian citizens, can enter visa-free for up to 90 days under the CA-4 agreement shared with Guatemala, Honduras, and Nicaragua.

▾Is El Salvador safe to visit now?

The country has undergone a significant, widely reported security transformation in recent years, and the main tourist circuits — San Salvador, the surf coast, and the Ruta de las Flores — now see regular tourism; as with anywhere, check current advisories before finalizing plans.

▾Why has El Salvador become a surf destination?

Its Pacific coast offers consistent, high-quality point breaks, particularly around El Tunco and El Zonte, which have drawn a growing international surf community alongside the country's improved safety reputation.

▾Does El Salvador use the US dollar?

Yes, El Salvador officially adopted the US dollar as its currency in 2001, which simplifies budgeting for American visitors specifically.

▾How many days does El Salvador need?

6-8 days lets you combine San Salvador, a volcano hike, the Ruta de las Flores highland towns, and a few days on the surf coast.
