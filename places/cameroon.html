Sometimes called 'Africa in miniature' for a landscape spanning desert, savanna, rainforest, and coastline within one country, a volcanic peak that's West Africa's tallest, and one of the continent's most linguistically diverse nations.

Travel smart

Bottled or purified water only. Not expected; small tips are appreciated for service.

Cameroon is often nicknamed 'Africa in miniature' because it contains nearly every major African ecosystem and landscape type within its borders — desert in the far north, savanna, dense rainforest, mountains, and Atlantic coastline — a geographic diversity matched by few other countries on the continent. It is also one of the most linguistically diverse nations in Africa, with over 250 distinct languages spoken alongside its two official languages, English and French, a legacy of its unusual dual British and French colonial history.

When to go

Dry season (north)November–March

The most comfortable window for the northern savanna and Waza National Park, with lower humidity and good wildlife visibility.

Dry season (south/coast)December–February

A shorter dry window in the south and along the coast, good for Mount Cameroon and Kribi's beaches.

Wet seasonApril–October

Heavy rain, particularly in the south and rainforest regions, though the landscape is at its lushest.

Transition periodsMarch, November

Shoulder months balancing rain risk with lower crowds nationally.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for Cameroon?

Most nationalities, including US, UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian citizens, need a visa or e-visa arranged in advance; only a small number of neighboring African countries are visa-exempt.

▾What does 'Africa in miniature' mean?

Cameroon contains nearly every major African ecosystem within its borders — desert, savanna, rainforest, mountains, and coastline — a geographic diversity that's rare to find in a single country.

▾Is Cameroon safe for tourists?

The southern and coastal tourist circuits around Yaoundé, Douala, and Kribi see regular tourism; it's worth checking current advisories closely for the northern Far North region and English-speaking Northwest/Southwest regions before finalizing an itinerary.

▾How many days does Cameroon need?

9-11 days lets you combine Mount Cameroon, the beaches around Kribi, and time in the rainforest or savanna regions, given the country's significant internal travel distances.

▾Why does Cameroon have two official languages?

A legacy of its unusual colonial history, split between British and French administration after WWI, resulting in English and French both holding official status alongside over 250 Indigenous languages.

▾How much does it cost to visit Waza National Park?

Foreign visitor entry runs roughly XAF 5,000-10,000 (about $8-17), plus a mandatory guide fee typically charged separately. It's one of Central Africa's better-known wildlife parks, with fees modest compared to those in East and Southern Africa.
