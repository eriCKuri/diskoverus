A futuristic Caspian Sea capital of flame-shaped towers built on oil wealth, a UNESCO-listed old city of medieval walls, and a landscape ranging from mud volcanoes to alpine peaks.

Travel smart

Bottled water recommended outside capital cities. 10% is appreciated, not required.

Baku's Flame Towers, illuminated nightly to mimic flickering fire, reference Azerbaijan's ancient association with 'eternal flames' fueled by underground natural gas seeps, a phenomenon tied to the country's status as a birthplace of Zoroastrian fire worship. Azerbaijan also holds one of the world's highest concentrations of mud volcanoes — over 300, roughly a third of the global total — a genuinely strange geological feature found in only a handful of places on Earth.

When to go

SpringApril–May

Mild and green, a good window for Baku and the countryside before summer heat sets in.

SummerJune–August

Hot in Baku and the lowlands, though the Caucasus foothills around Sheki and Gabala stay more comfortable.

AutumnSeptember–October

Comfortable temperatures nationwide, widely considered the best overall season for both Baku and the mountains.

WinterNovember–March

Mild on the Caspian coast but cold in the mountains — Shahdag offers a small but growing ski destination.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for Azerbaijan?

Most nationalities, including US, UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian citizens, need an e-visa applied for online via the ASAN Visa portal, typically approved within a few days and allowing stays up to 30 days.

▾Is Baku worth several days, or just a stopover?

Worth several days — the walled Old City, Flame Towers, and Caspian boulevard offer enough to fill 3-4 days before heading out to Sheki or Gabala in the countryside.

▾What are the mud volcanoes I keep hearing about?

Azerbaijan holds roughly a third of the world's mud volcanoes — over 300 — a genuinely strange geological phenomenon where gas pressure pushes cool mud to the surface, most accessible on day trips from Baku.

▾How many days does Azerbaijan need?

7-9 days lets you combine Baku's old and new city, a mud volcano excursion, and the mountain town of Sheki without rushing.

▾Is Azerbaijan safe to travel around?

The main tourist circuit — Baku, Sheki, and Gabala — sees regular safe tourism; as with neighboring Armenia, it's worth checking current advisories for border regions given ongoing regional tensions.

▾How much does it cost to visit Gobustan National Park?

Foreign visitor entry to see the ancient petroglyphs runs a modest AZN 3-5 (about $2-3), plus a separate small museum fee. It's an inexpensive half-day trip from Baku relative to many other capital-adjacent heritage sites.
