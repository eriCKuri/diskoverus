Things to do in Samoa

A Polynesian nation where traditional fa'a Samoa communal culture remains genuinely central to daily life, freshwater swimming holes formed by collapsed lava tubes, and a coastline still refreshingly free of mass development.

Travel smart

Bottled water recommended on smaller islands. Not customary.

Samoa's fa'a Samoa ('the Samoan way') is a genuinely lived communal social system governing land tenure, chiefly titles, and village life, still actively practiced rather than a ceremonial relic, making it one of the most intact traditional Polynesian societies remaining today. Its To Sua Ocean Trench, a collapsed lava tube filled with swimmable turquoise water roughly 30 metres below ground level, is one of the most photographed natural swimming holes in the entire Pacific.

When to go

Dry seasonMay–October

The most popular and comfortable window nationally, with lower humidity and reduced rainfall.

Wet seasonNovember–April

Warmer and more humid with regular rain, including the Pacific cyclone risk window.

Cyclone risk windowDecember–March

The highest tropical cyclone risk period, monitor forecasts closely if traveling in this window.

Transition periodsApril, November

Shoulder months balancing decent weather with fewer crowds between the two main seasons.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for Samoa?

Most Western passport holders, including US, UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian citizens, can enter visa-free for up to 60 days for tourism.

▾What is fa'a Samoa?

'The Samoan way,' a genuinely lived communal social system governing land tenure, chiefly titles, and village life, still actively practiced today rather than a ceremonial relic for visitors.

▾Upolu or Savai'i, which island should I prioritize?

Upolu has the capital Apia and most tourism infrastructure; Savai'i, Samoa's larger but less-developed island, offers a more traditional, slower pace and dramatic lava field landscapes.

▾How many days does Samoa need?

7-9 days lets you combine Upolu's beaches and To Sua Ocean Trench with a few days exploring Savai'i's villages and lava fields.

▾Is Samoa affected by cyclones?

Yes, particularly December through March, the peak Pacific cyclone risk window. Check current forecasts if traveling during this period.

▾How much does it cost to visit To Sua Ocean Trench?

Foreign visitor entry runs a modest WST 20-30 (about $7-11), collected by the local village that manages the site. Many of Samoa's natural attractions are on customary village land, so small community-managed entry fees like this one are common across the islands.
