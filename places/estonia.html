Things to do in Estonia

A digitally advanced small nation with a medieval old town at its core, dense pine forest covering half the country, and a startup culture that's outsized for its population.

Travel smart

Tap water is safe to drink. Not expected. Rounding up or 5–10% at restaurants is generous.

Estonia pioneered e-Residency and runs nearly all government services online, a level of digital infrastructure unusual for a country of just 1.3 million people, and it's produced more unicorn startups per capita than almost anywhere in Europe. Tallinn's Old Town is one of the best-preserved medieval city centers in Northern Europe, largely untouched by WWII bombing that leveled much of the rest of the region.

When to go

SpringApril–May

Snow melting and days lengthening quickly, with Tallinn's Old Town coming back to life.

SummerJune–August

The best window: mild temperatures, long daylight (near-endless around the solstice), and the busiest season.

AutumnSeptember–October

Crisp and colorful, with mushroom and berry foraging season in the forests.

WinterNovember–March

Cold, dark, and often snowy, Tallinn's Christmas market is a highlight, and the frozen landscape suits saunas and ice activities.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for Estonia?

US, UK, Canadian, and Australian passport holders can enter visa-free for up to 90 days within any 180-day period under Schengen rules.

▾Is Tallinn worth more than a day trip from Helsinki?

Yes. While many visitors do arrive on a ferry day trip from Helsinki, staying 2-3 nights allows time for the Old Town, the Kadriorg Palace and park, and a day trip into the countryside or to Estonia's second city, Tartu.

▾Is Estonia cheap compared to its Nordic neighbors?

Yes, noticeably: Estonia is significantly more affordable than Finland, Sweden, or Norway, while sharing much of the same clean, well-organized, digitally advanced infrastructure.

▾What's Estonia's e-Residency program, and does it affect visitors?

It's a digital identity program letting entrepreneurs anywhere run an EU-based business remotely, it doesn't affect tourism directly, but it's part of why Estonia has an outsized reputation as a tech-forward small nation.

▾Are Estonia's forests and nature reserves easy to visit without a car?

Some are accessible by public transit and organized tours from Tallinn, but a rental car opens up far more of the country's bogs, forests, and coastal areas, which make up roughly half of Estonia's land.

▾How much does it cost to visit Tallinn's key sights?

Tallinn's Old Town and most churches are free to walk through, though the TV Tower observation deck costs around €15 and a handful of museums charge modest separate fees, typically €8-12. A Tallinn Card can work out cheaper if you're planning to hit several paid sites in one visit.
