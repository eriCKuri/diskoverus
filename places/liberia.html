Travel smart

Bottled or purified water only. Not expected; small tips are appreciated.

Liberia is Africa's oldest modern republic, founded in 1847 by freed African American and Caribbean settlers with direct support from the American Colonization Society, giving it a national history, flag, and capital (Monrovia, named for US President James Monroe) more directly tied to the United States than any other African nation. Its Sapo National Park protects one of the last major stretches of the Upper Guinean rainforest, one of the most biodiverse and threatened forest ecosystems on Earth.

When to go

Dry seasonNovember–April

The most popular and comfortable window nationally, with clear skies ideal for the beaches and interior travel.

Early wet seasonMay–June

Increasing rain as the wet season builds, though still workable with flexible planning.

Peak wet seasonJuly–September

Extremely heavy rain, among the wettest climates in West Africa, with very limited tourism activity.

Transition periodOctober

Rains easing as the dry season approaches, a reasonable shoulder month.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for Liberia?

Most nationalities, including US, UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian citizens, need a visa arranged in advance through Liberian embassies or an e-visa where available.

▾Is Liberia safe to visit?

Monrovia and the main Western beach circuit see regular, generally safe tourism, though the country's tourism infrastructure remains limited following the civil war and 2014 Ebola outbreak — travel with an established operator is recommended, especially outside the capital.

▾Why does Liberia have such strong ties to the United States?

It was founded in 1847 by freed African American and Caribbean settlers with support from the American Colonization Society, giving it a national history, flag, and capital (Monrovia, named for US President James Monroe) more directly tied to the US than any other African nation.

▾How many days does Liberia need?

7-9 days lets you combine Monrovia's history with time along the coast, given the country's currently limited tourism infrastructure outside the capital region.

▾Does Liberia use the US dollar?

Yes, US dollars are widely used and accepted alongside the Liberian dollar, a legacy of the country's historical ties to the United States.

▾How much does it cost to visit Sapo National Park?

Foreign visitor entry runs a modest $10-25 USD, plus a mandatory guide fee, given the park's remote rainforest location. Liberia's tourism infrastructure is still developing, so arranging a visit typically means going through a specialist operator rather than showing up independently.
