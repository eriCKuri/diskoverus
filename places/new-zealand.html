Travel smart

Tap water is safe to drink. Not customary.

New Zealand was one of the last large landmasses on earth to be settled by people, arriving with Polynesian voyagers roughly seven hundred years ago, which is part of why its wildlife evolved so strangely, flightless birds with no mammal predators to avoid until humans and their animals showed up. Being in the Southern Hemisphere flips its seasons opposite the northern half of the world, which trips up more travellers than any packing list warns them about.

When to go

SummerDecember–February

Long, warm days and the busiest season, especially over the New Zealand school holidays around late December and January. Best for beaches and the Great Walks.

AutumnMarch–May

Stable weather, fewer crowds and colour changes in the South Island's beech forests, many consider this the best all-round window for hiking and driving.

WinterJune–August

Ski season around Queenstown, Wānaka and the central North Island, with snow closing some high mountain passes elsewhere.

SpringSeptember–November

Wildflowers and newborn lambs across the country, but also the wettest season in many regions. Pack for both sun and rain.

Adventure & outdoors

8

Hike a Great Walk

Nine officially maintained multi-day tracks, from the Milford Track's fjords to the Tongariro Northern Circuit's volcanic craters, huts need booking well ahead in summer.

adventuremultiOct–Apr for most, check individual track conditions

Bungee jump where it began

AJ Hackett's Kawarau Bridge near Queenstown was the world's first commercial bungee site: still running the original jump alongside newer, higher ones nearby.

adventuremorningYear-round

Walk on a glacier in South Westland

Fox and Franz Josef glaciers descend unusually close to sea level through rainforest: access changes yearly as the ice retreats, so guided helicopter trips are now standard.

adventurehalfYear-round, weather-dependent

Drive the Milford Road$130–285

One of the world's great scenic drives, from Te Anau to Milford Sound through the Hollyford and Eglinton valleys, past the Homer Tunnel.

adventurefullYear-round, watch for winter chains

Skydive over Lake Taupō or Queenstown

Two of the most scenic drop zones on earth: one over a volcanic lake, the other over the Remarkables mountain range and Lake Wakatipu.

adventurehalfYear-round, weather-dependent

Ski or snowboard the Southern Alps

Queenstown and Wānaka anchor the country's main ski fields, with terrain ranging from beginner slopes to genuine backcountry.

adventurefullJun–Sep

Walk the Tongariro Alpine Crossing

A one-day, nineteen-kilometre trail across active volcanic terrain: emerald lakes, a crater rim and, on a clear day, views to Mount Taranaki.

adventurefullNov–Apr, closes with snow

Jet boat a canyon river

Shallow-draft boats built for New Zealand's braided rivers, spinning through canyons at speed, Shotover River near Queenstown is the best known.

Common questions

▾Is New Zealand safe to visit?

Yes. New Zealand is one of the safest countries in the world for tourists, with very low crime rates. The bigger practical risks come from outdoor activities and changeable weather on hikes and drives, so checking conditions before heading out matters more than typical safety concerns.

▾How much does it cost to visit Milford Sound?

There's no separate entrance fee to Milford Sound itself: the fiord, the free Lookout Track and the roadside viewpoints all cost nothing. What you pay for is a cruise, typically NZD 180-250 for a standard daytime sailing, or a coach-plus-cruise day tour from Queenstown running roughly $130-285 depending on the operator and inclusions.

▾How many days do you need in New Zealand?

14-16 days lets you cover both islands at a reasonable pace: Auckland and the north, then Queenstown, Milford Sound and the south. A shorter 10-day trip usually means focusing on just the South Island, which has the bulk of the classic scenery.

▾Do I need a visa for New Zealand?

Most visa-exempt nationalities, including the US, UK, Canada and EU, need to apply for a New Zealand Electronic Travel Authority (NZeTA) in advance for a small fee, plus an International Visitor Conservation and Tourism Levy that funds parks and infrastructure. Both are quick online applications, not a traditional visa.

▾What's the best time to visit New Zealand?

December through February is New Zealand's summer and the most popular time to visit, with the longest days and best access to alpine trails. The shoulder seasons of October-November and March-April offer a good weather-to-crowd tradeoff.

▾Is New Zealand expensive?

New Zealand reads as a $$$ destination: accommodation, car rental and guided activities like fjord cruises or glacier hikes add up quickly. Self-driving with a mix of campervan and budget lodging is the most common way travelers control costs.
