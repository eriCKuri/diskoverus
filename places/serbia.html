Things to do in Serbia

A Balkan capital built at the confluence of two rivers with a fortress overlooking both, a trumpet festival that turns a small town into Europe's loudest party, and a genuinely underrated food and nightlife scene.

Travel smart

Bottled water recommended outside capital cities. 10% is common.

Belgrade's Kalemegdan Fortress sits at the confluence of the Danube and Sava rivers, a strategically vital position fought over roughly 115 times across its history, making it one of the most contested pieces of ground in Europe. Serbia's Guča Trumpet Festival draws hundreds of thousands of visitors each year to a small town for a competitive brass band tradition unlike anything else on the continent, reflecting a distinctly Balkan musical heritage.

When to go

SpringApril–May

Mild and green, a good window for Belgrade and Novi Sad before summer heat and crowds.

SummerJune–August

Hot, with Belgrade's riverside clubs and festivals — including EXIT Festival in Novi Sad — at their peak.

AutumnSeptember–October

Comfortable temperatures and the Guča Trumpet Festival, widely considered a strong all-around season.

WinterNovember–March

Cold with mountain snow — Kopaonik offers an affordable, well-regarded ski destination by European standards.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for Serbia?

US, UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian passport holders can enter visa-free for up to 90 days. Serbia isn't in the Schengen Area, so it maintains its own separate entry rules.

▾Is Belgrade's nightlife really as good as its reputation?

Yes, widely regarded as one of Europe's best underrated nightlife scenes, especially its floating river clubs (splavovi) moored along the Danube and Sava, drawing visitors specifically for the scene.

▾What's the Guča Trumpet Festival?

An annual competitive brass band festival in a small western Serbian town that draws hundreds of thousands of visitors for days of nonstop live trumpet music, considered one of the most distinctive cultural events in the Balkans.

▾How many days does Serbia need?

6-8 days lets you combine Belgrade, Novi Sad, and either a mountain excursion to Zlatibor or a monastery route through central Serbia.

▾Is Serbia affordable?

Yes, among the more budget-friendly destinations in the Balkans, with strong value across food, nightlife, and accommodation.

▾How much does it cost to visit Belgrade Fortress and Novi Sad's Petrovaradin?

Belgrade Fortress and Kalemegdan Park are free to walk through, with only a handful of smaller on-site museums charging modest separate fees, typically RSD 200-400 (about $2-4). Petrovaradin Fortress in Novi Sad is similarly free to explore on foot.
