Things to do in Malawi

Nicknamed the 'Warm Heart of Africa' for a hospitality reputation that consistently surprises visitors, a freshwater lake holding more fish species than any other lake on Earth, and safari country without the crowds.

Travel smart

Bottled water recommended outside major cities. 10% at restaurants; guides and drivers appreciate a daily tip.

Lake Malawi holds more fish species than any other lake in the world — estimates range into the hundreds of endemic cichlid species alone — making it one of the most biologically significant freshwater ecosystems on the planet and a UNESCO World Heritage site. Malawi has long been nicknamed the 'Warm Heart of Africa,' a reputation for genuine hospitality that consistently ranks among visitors' most-cited impressions of the country, distinguishing it from more heavily marketed regional safari destinations.

When to go

Dry, cool seasonMay–August

The most popular and comfortable window nationally, with clear skies and cooler temperatures ideal for safari and lake activities.

Hot, dry seasonSeptember–October

Rising temperatures ahead of the rains, still workable for wildlife viewing as animals cluster around water sources.

Wet seasonNovember–March

Heavy rain nationwide, with lush green landscapes but more difficult road conditions in some areas.

Transition periodApril

Rains easing as the dry season approaches, a reasonable shoulder month with fewer crowds.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for Malawi?

US, UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian passport holders can get an e-visa online, allowing stays up to 30 days.

▾Why is Lake Malawi so significant for fish diversity?

It holds more fish species than any other lake on Earth, with estimates into the hundreds of endemic cichlid species alone, making it one of the most biologically significant freshwater ecosystems in the world and a UNESCO World Heritage site.

▾Is Malawi a good safari destination compared to its neighbors?

Yes, increasingly so — parks like Liwonde and Majete offer strong wildlife viewing, including reintroduced lions and rhinos, with far fewer crowds and lower prices than more heavily marketed destinations like Tanzania or Kenya.

▾How many days does Malawi need?

9-11 days lets you combine time on Lake Malawi with a safari at Liwonde or Majete National Park, given the country's compact size relative to other African safari destinations.

▾Why is Malawi called the 'Warm Heart of Africa'?

A long-standing nickname reflecting the country's genuine reputation for hospitality, consistently cited by visitors as one of their strongest impressions relative to other African destinations.
