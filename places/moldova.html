Things to do in Moldova

Europe's least-visited wine country holding some of the largest underground cellars on Earth, a breakaway territory frozen in Soviet time, and a genuinely under-the-radar countryside.

Travel smart

Bottled water recommended outside capital cities. 10% is common in restaurants.

Moldova's Mileștii Mici cellar holds the Guinness World Record for the largest wine collection on Earth, with roughly 200 kilometres of underground tunnels used both for storage and, unusually, as internal roads that visitors tour by car. The breakaway region of Transnistria, unrecognized by any UN member state and still displaying Soviet symbols and statues of Lenin, offers a strange and genuine glimpse of frozen Cold War-era atmosphere just a short trip from the capital.

When to go

SpringApril–May

Mild and green, a good window for Chișinău and the countryside before summer heat.

SummerJune–August

Warm and the best window for outdoor wine touring and countryside travel.

AutumnSeptember–October

Harvest season across Moldova's wine regions and comfortable temperatures — widely considered the best season.

WinterNovember–March

Cold, with a quiet tourism season nationally outside the underground wine cellars, which stay a constant temperature year-round.

Food & drink

8

Tour the underground wine tunnels of Mileștii Mici

A Guinness World Record holder for the largest wine collection on Earth, with roughly 200 kilometres of tunnels driven through by car during the tour.

foodfullYear-round, constant cellar temperature

Explore Cricova's underground wine city

Another vast underground cellar network, with street names and traffic signs inside its tunnels, home to bottles reportedly including Nazi-looted wine recovered after WWII.

foodfullYear-round

Eat mămăligă, Moldova's cornmeal staple

A polenta-like dish typically served with cheese, sour cream, and stew, reflecting Moldova's shared culinary heritage with neighboring Romania.

foodeveningYear-round

Taste Moldovan wine at a countryside vineyard

Moldova has one of the highest vine-per-capita ratios in the world, with a wine culture predating the Roman Empire and family-run vineyards open for tastings.

foodfullYear-round, harvest Sept–Oct

Try Moldovan plăcintă pastries

Thin-layered pastries filled with cheese, potato, cabbage, or fruit, a genuine everyday staple sold at bakeries and markets nationwide.

foodmorningYear-round

Visit the Purcari winery near the Ukrainian border

One of Moldova's oldest and most internationally recognized wineries, whose wines were reportedly served to European royalty in the 19th century.

foodfullYear-round

Try Moldovan brânză cheese and zeamă soup

A tangy sheep's-milk cheese and a sour chicken noodle soup, both reflecting Moldova's rustic, dairy- and herb-forward home cooking.

foodeveningYear-round

Attend the Chișinău Wine Festival (if timed right)

An annual national celebration of Moldovan winemaking held in the capital's central square each autumn, drawing producers from across the country.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for Moldova?

US, UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian passport holders can enter visa-free for up to 90 days. Moldova isn't in the Schengen Area, so it maintains its own separate entry rules.

▾Is it safe and legal to visit Transnistria?

Yes, day trips are commonly done and generally safe, though it's a breakaway territory not recognized by any UN member state — bring your passport, expect a basic entry registration process, and check current advisories given regional tensions before planning a visit.

▾Is the Mileștii Mici wine cellar really the world's largest?

Yes, it holds the Guinness World Record for the largest wine collection on Earth, with roughly 200 kilometres of underground tunnels used both for storage and as internal roads toured by car.

▾How many days does Moldova need?

5-7 days covers Chișinău, a wine cellar tour, and a Transnistria day trip comfortably, reflecting the country's small size.

▾Is Moldova affordable?

Yes, considered one of the most budget-friendly countries in Europe, with strong value across food, wine, and accommodation.
