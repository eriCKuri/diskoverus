Things to do in Lesotho

The only country in the world entirely above 1,000 metres elevation, a mountain kingdom completely surrounded by South Africa, and a horseback riding and pony trekking culture unlike anywhere else in Africa.

Travel smart

Bottled water recommended outside major cities. 10% at restaurants; guides and drivers appreciate a daily tip.

Lesotho is the only sovereign country in the world with its entire territory situated above 1,000 metres in elevation, earning it the nickname the 'Kingdom in the Sky,' and it is one of only three countries entirely surrounded by a single other nation (in Lesotho's case, South Africa). Its Basotho pony trekking tradition, developed out of necessity in a mountainous country with limited road access, remains a genuinely practical form of transport as well as one of the most distinctive adventure tourism activities in Southern Africa.

When to go

SummerNovember–February

Warm days with regular afternoon thunderstorms, and the most accessible window for high mountain trekking.

AutumnMarch–April

Comfortable temperatures and clearing skies, a strong window before winter cold sets in at altitude.

WinterMay–August

Cold, often with snow at higher elevations: Afriski offers genuine skiing, a rarity in sub-Saharan Africa.

SpringSeptember–October

Mild and drying out, a good transitional window before the summer rains begin.

Adventure & outdoors

8

Go Basotho pony trekking through the mountains

A traditional form of transport in this famously mountainous country, now one of the most distinctive adventure activities in Southern Africa.

adventuremultiSeptember–April

Drive or hike the Sani Pass into the Maluti Mountains

One of the most dramatic mountain pass roads in Africa, connecting South Africa's KwaZulu-Natal to Lesotho's high plateau via steep switchbacks.

adventurefullSeptember–April, driest

Ski or snowboard at Afriski Mountain Resort

A genuine ski resort in the high Maluti Mountains, a rarity anywhere in sub-Saharan Africa given the region's typically warm climate.

adventurefullJune–August

Hike in Sehlabathebe National Park$5–10

A UNESCO World Heritage remote highland park with dramatic sandstone formations, San rock art, and rare high-altitude wildlife.

One of the highest single-drop waterfalls in Southern Africa, also home to the world's longest single-drop commercial abseil.

A high-altitude park with one of Lesotho's few remaining stands of indigenous forest, offering trekking to a mountain lodge.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for Lesotho?

Most Western passport holders, including US, UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian citizens, can enter visa-free for up to 90 days for tourism.

▾Is Lesotho part of South Africa?

No. It's a fully independent sovereign kingdom, though it's entirely surrounded by South African territory, one of only three countries in the world with this geographic distinction.

▾Can you really ski in Lesotho?

Yes. Afriski, in the high Maluti Mountains, offers a genuine ski season, a rarity anywhere in sub-Saharan Africa, reflecting Lesotho's status as the only country entirely above 1,000 metres elevation.

▾How many days does Lesotho need?

6-8 days lets you combine a multi-day Basotho pony trek with time in Maseru and the Sani Pass region, given the country's mountainous terrain slows overland travel.

▾Is Lesotho often combined with a South Africa trip?

Yes, very commonly, its position entirely within South African territory makes it a natural and relatively easy add-on to a wider Drakensberg or KwaZulu-Natal itinerary.

▾How much does it cost to visit Sehlabathebe National Park?

Foreign visitor entry runs a modest fee of around $5-10 USD, among the more affordable park fees in Southern Africa. Most visitors combine a Lesotho trip with pony trekking or hiking excursions, which are priced separately from park entry.
