A country that stretches from subtropical north to Patagonian ice, with Buenos Aires holding it all together.

Travel smart

Bottled or filtered water recommended. 10% at restaurants is common.

Argentina spans nearly 3,700 kilometres from subtropical Iguazu Falls to sub-Antarctic Ushuaia, the southernmost city on Earth. Buenos Aires has more theatre seats per capita than almost any city outside New York or London, and Argentines eat more beef per person than nearly anywhere else in the world.

When to go

Patagonia summerDecember–February

The only realistic window for full trail access on the Fitz Roy and Torres del Paine circuits — also the busiest and most expensive months down south.

Buenos Aires shoulderSeptember–November, March–May

Mild, pleasant weather in the capital and wine country, avoiding both summer humidity and winter chill.

Ski seasonJune–September

Southern-hemisphere winter turns Bariloche and Las Leñas into full ski resorts, opposite the northern hemisphere's calendar.

Iguazu FallsYear-round, best September–November and March–May

The falls run year-round, but shoulder-season months avoid both the summer heat and peak holiday crowds.

Common questions

▾Is Argentina safe to visit?

Yes — Argentina's main tourist circuit, Buenos Aires, Patagonia and Iguazu Falls, is generally safe with normal precautions. Petty theft in busy areas of Buenos Aires is the more common issue than anything more serious.

▾How much does it cost to visit Iguazu Falls?

Entry to the Argentine side of Iguazu National Park costs roughly $22-32 for international adult visitors, noticeably more than the $18 charged on the Brazilian side of the same falls. Many visitors cross the border to see both angles, in which case both entry fees apply separately.

▾How many days do you need in Argentina?

12-14 days is realistic given the country's size — a few days in Buenos Aires, a trip to Iguazu Falls, and time in Patagonia if your itinerary allows, since domestic flights between regions eat into shorter trips. A one-week trip usually means picking just Buenos Aires and Iguazu.

▾Do I need a visa for Argentina?

US, UK, Canadian, EU and most other passport holders can enter visa-free for up to 90 days as tourists. Check the current list for your specific nationality, since a smaller number of countries do need a visa arranged in advance.

▾What's the best time to visit Argentina?

This depends heavily on the region — Buenos Aires and Iguazu are pleasant nearly year-round with December-February as their hottest months, while Patagonia's trekking season runs roughly November through March, its own summer, and is largely inaccessible outside that window.

▾Is Argentina expensive?

Argentina has swung between being a bargain and a pricier destination depending on the peso's exchange rate and inflation in a given year, so costs can shift meaningfully within a single year. Checking a current exchange rate close to your travel dates gives a much better read than any fixed guide.
