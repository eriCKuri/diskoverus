Things to do in Israel

Three faiths' holiest sites within walking distance of each other in Jerusalem's Old City, a floating swim in the Dead Sea, and a Mediterranean coastline with a genuinely modern, secular counterpoint in Tel Aviv.

Jerusalem's Old City packs the Western Wall, the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, and the Dome of the Rock into less than a square kilometre, making it one of the most historically dense religious sites anywhere on Earth. The Dead Sea, at over 430 metres below sea level, is the lowest point on land, with mineral-rich water so buoyant that swimming there is closer to floating in an armchair.

When to go

SpringMarch–May

Mild and blooming, one of the most comfortable windows for visiting both Jerusalem and the desert.

SummerJune–August

Hot and dry, especially inland and in the south; the Mediterranean coast stays humid but the beaches are at their best.

AutumnSeptember–November

Warm days easing into comfortable temperatures, another strong window for touring.

WinterDecember–February

Mild on the coast, occasional cold snaps and even rare snow in Jerusalem; the Dead Sea and Eilat stay warm.

Culture & history

9

Walk Jerusalem's Old City through all four quarters

The Jewish, Christian, Muslim, and Armenian quarters sit within the same walled kilometre, each with a distinct character and centuries of layered history.

Pray or observe at the Western Wall

Judaism's holiest accessible site, where visitors of any faith can approach and, if they choose, leave a written prayer in the stone's cracks.

Explore Tel Aviv's Bauhaus 'White City' architecture

Over 4,000 Bauhaus-style buildings from the 1930s, a UNESCO-listed concentration unmatched anywhere else in the world.

Visit the Church of the Holy Sepulchre

Traditionally venerated as the site of Christ's crucifixion and burial, shared and jointly maintained by several Christian denominations.

Tour Yad Vashem, Israel's Holocaust memorial and museum

A comprehensive and carefully designed memorial and museum in Jerusalem, widely regarded as among the most significant Holocaust institutions in the world.

Explore the Old City of Akko (Acre)

A UNESCO-listed Crusader and Ottoman-era port city with underground tunnels, a working fishing harbor, and far fewer visitors than Jerusalem.

Visit the ancient port city ruins at Caesarea

A Roman-built harbor city later expanded by Herod the Great, with a restored amphitheater still used for concerts against a Mediterranean backdrop.

Explore the Mount of Olives and its panoramic view of Jerusalem

An ancient Jewish cemetery and several key Christian sites sit on this ridge, with the classic postcard view over the Old City and Temple Mount.

Home to the Dead Sea Scrolls, some of the oldest surviving biblical manuscripts, displayed in a purpose-built structure shaped like the jars they were found in.

Adventure & outdoors

4

Climb Masada for sunrise

A clifftop desert fortress overlooking the Dead Sea, reachable by cable car or the steep Snake Path, best climbed before dawn to beat the heat.

adventuremorningYear-round, avoid midday heat

Snorkel or dive the coral reefs of Eilat

Israel's Red Sea resort city, with accessible coral reef diving and snorkeling just off the beach, unusual for a country otherwise defined by the Mediterranean and desert.

adventurefullYear-round, warmest March–November

Hike the Israel National Trail's northern sections in the Galilee

Rolling hills, forests, and small kibbutz communities in Israel's greenest region, a world away from the desert south.

adventurefullMarch–May, October–November

Ride a jeep or camel into the Negev Desert

Israel's southern desert, with craters, Bedouin hospitality tents, and stargazing far from any city light.

adventurefullOctober–April, avoid summer heat

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for Israel?

Citizens of around 96 visa-exempt countries, including the US, UK, EU, Canada, and Australia, still need to apply for an ETA-IL electronic travel authorization online before flying, ideally at least 72 hours ahead — it's a quick digital process, not a traditional visa.

▾Is it safe to travel to Israel right now?

Conditions can change, and different regions carry different risk levels at different times, so check your government's current travel advisory close to your departure date rather than relying on older information, and stay flexible with your itinerary.

▾How many days does Jerusalem's Old City need?

A full day covers the highlights, but two days lets you properly explore all four quarters (Jewish, Christian, Muslim, and Armenian) without rushing, plus visit the City of David and Mount of Olives.

▾Is the Dead Sea worth a special trip?

Yes — the extreme buoyancy and mineral mud are a genuinely unique experience, and it pairs naturally with a visit to nearby Masada, best climbed before sunrise to avoid the heat.

▾Is Tel Aviv very different from Jerusalem?

Very much so — Tel Aviv is a modern, secular, beach-city counterpoint to Jerusalem's religious and historical density, known for Bauhaus architecture, nightlife, and a strong food scene.
