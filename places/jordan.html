Travel smart

Bottled water recommended. 10–15% expected, often added as a service charge.

Jordan is one of the more stable and tourist-friendly countries in the Middle East, and it packs an outsized range of landscape into a small footprint — you can stand in Wadi Rum's Mars-red desert in the morning and float in the Dead Sea, the lowest point on earth's land surface, by afternoon. Petra alone was voted one of the New Seven Wonders of the World and still isn't fully excavated.

When to go

SpringMarch–May

Widely considered Petra and Wadi Rum's best season — mild temperatures, clear light, and occasional desert wildflowers.

SummerJune–August

Intense heat, often exceeding 35°C at Petra and higher in the Dead Sea valley — manageable with an early start, and noticeably quieter.

AutumnSeptember–November

A second sweet spot with fewer crowds than spring and similarly comfortable temperatures.

WinterDecember–February

Cold at Petra's elevation, sometimes near freezing at night, with occasional snow — the quietest and cheapest season by far.

Common questions

▾Is Jordan safe to visit?

Yes — Jordan is one of the more stable countries in the region and is considered safe for tourists, with a strong security presence around Petra, Wadi Rum and Amman. Areas near the Syrian and Iraqi borders carry travel advisories and aren't part of typical itineraries anyway.

▾How much does it cost to visit Petra?

A one-day ticket for visitors staying overnight in Jordan costs 50 JOD (about $70), rising to 55 JOD for two days and 60 JOD for three — Petra rewards more than one day, so the multi-day tickets are worth it if your schedule allows. Visitors who aren't staying overnight in the country pay a steeper 90 JOD single-day rate, and Jordanian citizens pay a fraction of that, around 1 JOD.

▾How many days do you need in Jordan?

7-10 days lets you cover Petra properly (two days is worthwhile), Wadi Rum, the Dead Sea and Amman without rushing. A shorter 5-day trip can still work if you focus on just Petra and Wadi Rum.

▾Do I need a visa for Jordan?

Most nationalities need a visa — a standard single-entry visa at the border costs 40 JOD, though it's often better value to buy the Jordan Pass instead, which bundles the visa fee with entry to Petra and dozens of other sites for roughly 70-80 JOD depending on the tier, as long as you're staying at least three nights.

▾Is the Jordan Pass worth it?

For most visitors, yes. The cheapest Jordan Pass tier alone is close to the cost of a standalone one-day Petra ticket plus the visa fee, and it also covers Wadi Rum, Jerash and dozens of smaller sites. It only stops making sense if you're skipping Petra entirely, which very few Jordan itineraries do.

▾Is Jordan expensive?

Jordan reads as a mid-range destination — Petra's entry fee and desert camp stays in Wadi Rum push costs above neighboring Egypt, but it's still less expensive than a comparable trip in Western Europe. The Jordan Pass is the single biggest lever for keeping costs down if you're visiting multiple sites.
