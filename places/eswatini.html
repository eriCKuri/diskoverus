Things to do in Eswatini

One of the last absolute monarchies left in the world, a reed dance ceremony drawing tens of thousands of participants, and a small kingdom offering genuine safari without the crowds of its larger neighbors.

Bottled water recommended outside major cities. 10% at restaurants; guides and drivers appreciate a daily tip.

Eswatini is one of the last remaining absolute monarchies in the world, where King Mswati III holds direct governing authority alongside the traditional structures of the Swazi nation, a political system now rare on a global scale. Its annual Umhlanga (Reed Dance) ceremony draws tens of thousands of unmarried women in a multi-day cultural celebration honoring the Queen Mother, one of the largest and most visually striking traditional ceremonies still actively practiced anywhere in Africa.

When to go

Dry, cool seasonMay–August

The most comfortable window nationally, with clear skies and cooler temperatures ideal for wildlife viewing.

Warm, dry seasonSeptember–October

Rising temperatures ahead of the rains, still a strong window for wildlife as animals cluster around water sources.

Wet, hot seasonNovember–March

Regular rain and warm temperatures, with lush green landscapes across the highveld and lowveld.

Transition periodApril

Rains easing as the dry season approaches, a reasonable shoulder month.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for Eswatini?

Most Western passport holders, including US, UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian citizens, can enter visa-free for up to 30 days for tourism.

▾Is Eswatini the same as Swaziland?

Yes — the country's official name was changed from Swaziland to Eswatini in 2018 by royal decree, though the older name is still occasionally used or referenced.

▾Can visitors attend the Reed Dance ceremony?

Yes, portions of the multi-day Umhlanga ceremony are open to respectful visitors, offering a rare chance to witness one of the largest traditional cultural ceremonies still actively practiced in Africa.

▾How many days does Eswatini need?

5-7 days lets you combine a wildlife reserve visit, Mbabane, and cultural sites without rushing, given the country's small and manageable size.

▾Is Eswatini easy to combine with a South Africa trip?

Yes, very commonly — its position bordering South Africa's Mpumalanga and KwaZulu-Natal regions, near Kruger National Park, makes it a natural and easy add-on to a wider Southern Africa itinerary.

▾How much does it cost to visit Hlane Royal National Park?

Foreign visitor entry runs roughly SZL 100-150 (about $5-8) per person, plus a separate vehicle fee. It's one of the more affordable Big Five parks in Southern Africa, especially compared to South Africa's Kruger.
