Things to do in the UAE

Record-breaking skyscrapers and man-made islands beside centuries-old souks and desert dunes, built at a pace and scale unlike almost anywhere else.

Travel smart

Bottled water recommended. 10–15% expected, often added as a service charge.

The UAE turned a stretch of desert coastline into two of the world's most visited cities in roughly two generations, home to the tallest building on earth and an increasingly serious contemporary art and culinary scene. It's also one of the easiest Middle Eastern countries for first-time visitors to the region, with modern infrastructure and a genuinely international population — more than 80% of residents are foreign nationals.

When to go

Winter (peak)December–February

The best window — warm days, cool evenings, and the region's main outdoor festival and event season.

SpringMarch–April

Still comfortable and increasingly popular as a shoulder season, before temperatures climb sharply.

SummerMay–September

Brutally hot, regularly above 40°C — most life moves indoors or to air-conditioned malls and pools.

AutumnOctober–November

Heat starts easing by November, making it a reasonable if still-warm shoulder season.

Culture & history

9

Go up the Burj Khalifa's observation deck

The world's tallest building, with an observation deck at level 148 — book sunset slots well ahead, as they sell out fastest.

Explore the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque in Abu Dhabi

One of the world's largest mosques, with hand-knotted carpets, floral marble inlay, and a scale meant to be genuinely awe-inducing, free to visit with modest dress.

Wander the Dubai Gold and Spice Souks

Old Dubai's trading heart, where gold is sold by weight in shop windows and spice stalls pile saffron and frankincense in open sacks.

Visit the Louvre Abu Dhabi

A striking domed building on Saadiyat Island, showing art and artifacts spanning civilizations under a 'rain of light' perforated ceiling.

Cross the Dubai Creek by traditional abra boat

Wooden ferries have crossed the creek between Deira and Bur Dubai for generations, still the cheapest and most atmospheric way across.

Walk Alserkal Avenue's contemporary art galleries

A converted industrial district in Dubai now home to the city's most serious contemporary art scene, well outside the tourist center.

Visit Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood

Dubai's oldest surviving district, with wind-tower architecture, narrow lanes, and the Dubai Museum housed in a 19th-century fort.

Watch falconry at a Bedouin heritage camp

Falconry remains a genuine point of Emirati cultural pride, demonstrated at heritage villages and desert camps alongside traditional dance.

Explore Sharjah's museums and heritage district

The UAE's designated cultural capital, with more museums per capita than its flashier neighbors and a strictly enforced alcohol-free policy.

Adventure & outdoors

6

Ride a camel or dune-bash into the Dubai desert

Sunset desert safaris combine 4x4 dune bashing with camel rides, a Bedouin-style camp dinner, and stargazing away from city light.

adventureeveningYear-round, cooler Oct–April

Ski indoors at Ski Dubai

A full indoor snow slope inside a desert-city mall, complete with penguins and real snowfall — as strange as it sounds and genuinely fun.

adventurehalfYear-round

Snorkel or dive at Fujairah's coral coast

The UAE's only Indian Ocean-facing coastline, with calmer, clearer water for diving and snorkeling than the Gulf side.

adventurefullOctober–April

Hike the Hajar Mountains near Ras Al Khaimah

The UAE's highest terrain, with Jebel Jais offering hiking trails and the world's longest zipline for those wanting more adrenaline.

adventurefullNovember–March

Ride the world's fastest roller coaster at Ferrari World

Abu Dhabi's Formula Rossa reaches around 240 km/h, the centerpiece of an entire theme park built around the Ferrari brand.

adventurefullYear-round, indoor sections avoid heat

Take a helicopter or seaplane tour over Palm Jumeirah

The clearest way to appreciate the scale of Dubai's man-made islands, visible properly only from the air.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for the UAE?

Most Western passport holders, including US, UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian citizens, receive visa-free or visa-on-arrival entry for 30 to 90 days depending on nationality — check the specific allowance for your passport before booking.

▾Are there cultural rules I should know before visiting?

The UAE is more liberal than many Gulf neighbors but is still a Muslim country with local laws — dress modestly outside resorts and beach clubs, avoid public displays of affection, and be aware that alcohol is only served in licensed venues.

▾Is Dubai or Abu Dhabi better for a first visit?

Dubai has more of the iconic sights (Burj Khalifa, Palm Jumeirah, the souks) and nightlife; Abu Dhabi has the Louvre and Sheikh Zayed Mosque and feels calmer and more spread out. Many trips combine both over 5-7 days.

▾Is the UAE walkable or do I need to rent a car?

Dubai and Abu Dhabi are heavily car-oriented and built around highways, though both have metro systems covering key tourist areas. Taxis and rideshare are cheap and easy, so a rental car isn't essential for most visitors.

▾Is the UAE worth visiting outside of winter?

Only if you plan around the heat — summer temperatures regularly exceed 40°C, making outdoor activities impractical. December through March is by far the most comfortable window.

▾How much does it cost to visit the top of the Burj Khalifa?

Standard 'At the Top' tickets for levels 124-125 start around AED 169 (about $46) during non-peak hours, rising to roughly AED 219 for sunset time slots. Higher floor access (level 148) and skip-the-line options cost significantly more on top of the base price.
