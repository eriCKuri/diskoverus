Travel smart

Bottled or purified water only. Not customary. Rounding up is common.

Angkor Archaeological Park covers roughly 400 square kilometres and includes Angkor Wat, the largest religious monument on Earth, built by the Khmer Empire at its height in the 12th century. Cambodia's history since then, including the Khmer Rouge genocide of the 1970s, adds a weight and resilience to the country that shapes how many visitors experience it.

When to go

Cool dry season

November–February

The most comfortable and popular window: lower humidity, minimal rain, ideal for temple exploring.

Hot dry season

March–May

Intense heat, often exceeding 35°C, with temples best visited at dawn before the midday sun.

Early wet season

June–August

Regular but often brief afternoon rain, with lush green landscapes and Angkor's moats full.

Late wet season

September–October

The wettest months, with possible flooding on rural roads, the Tonlé Sap lake is at its largest and most dramatic.

Culture & history

Watch sunrise over Angkor Wat

The largest religious monument on Earth, its five towers reflected in a moat pool at dawn, arrive well before sunrise to claim a spot at the classic viewpoint.

culture · morning · Year-round, coolest Nov–Feb · $37

Explore the tree-swallowed ruins of Ta Prohm

Massive silk-cotton and strangler fig roots have grown through and around the temple's stonework, left deliberately unrestored for atmospheric effect.

culture · half day · Year-round

Visit the smiling stone faces of Bayon Temple

216 enormous carved faces gaze outward from the temple's towers, part of the walled city of Angkor Thom just north of Angkor Wat.

culture · half day · Year-round

Cruise Tonlé Sap Lake's floating villages

Southeast Asia's largest freshwater lake, home to entire villages, schools, and markets built on stilts or floating platforms.

culture · full day · June–October, lake fullest

Tour the Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum in Phnom Penh

A former high school converted into a Khmer Rouge prison, now a sobering memorial and museum documenting the regime's atrocities.

culture · half day · Year-round

Explore lesser-visited Angkor temples like Banteay Srei

A smaller, pink sandstone temple famed for its intricate, unusually well-preserved carvings, a quieter contrast to Angkor Wat's crowds.

culture · half day · Year-round · $37

Walk Phnom Penh's Royal Palace and Silver Pagoda

The current king's residence, with a pagoda floor laid in over 5,000 silver tiles and a Buddha statue encrusted with nearly 10,000 diamonds.

culture · half day · Year-round

Visit the Cambodian Landmine Museum near Siem Reap

Founded by a former child soldier turned deminer, documenting Cambodia's decades-long landmine clearance effort with striking personal testimony.

culture · half day · Year-round

Watch classical Apsara dance in Siem Reap

A centuries-old court dance form depicted on Angkor's temple carvings, still performed nightly at restaurants and cultural centers.

culture · evening · Year-round

Explore the Killing Fields at Choeung Ek

A memorial site outside Phnom Penh documenting the Khmer Rouge genocide, with an audio guide providing survivor testimony throughout.

culture · half day · Year-round

Visit Wat Phnom, Phnom Penh's founding legend site

A hilltop pagoda said to mark the founding of the city, with a small park and monkeys around its base in the heart of downtown.

culture · morning · Year-round

Support a local NGO project or social enterprise restaurant

Cambodia has a strong network of training restaurants and craft workshops supporting vulnerable youth and communities, offering a meaningful add-on to any itinerary.

culture · half day · Year-round

Adventure & outdoors

Cycle the rural roads around Battambang

Cambodia's rice-growing heartland, with a bamboo train, colonial-era shophouses, and a slower pace than Siem Reap or Phnom Penh.

adventure · full day · November–February

Trek to remote temple ruins at Preah Vihear

A dramatically situated temple perched on a cliff edge near the Thai border, requiring more effort to reach but rewarding with far fewer crowds.

adventure · full day · November–February

Food & drink

Eat amok, Cambodia's signature fish curry

Fish steamed in banana leaf with coconut, lemongrass, and kroeung spice paste: a defining Khmer dish, distinct from Thai curry despite similar ingredients.

food · evening · Year-round

Explore the coastal town of Kampot and its pepper farms

Kampot pepper has an international reputation among chefs, and riverside farms outside town offer tastings alongside relaxed countryside scenery.

food · full day · Year-round

Eat street food at Phnom Penh's Night Market

Riverside stalls selling grilled skewers, num banh chok noodles, and fresh tropical fruit, with mats laid out for casual outdoor dining.

food · evening · Year-round

Try Cambodian barbecue (phnom pleung)

A tabletop grill-your-own meal of marinated meats and vegetables, a popular casual dinner format in both Phnom Penh and Siem Reap.

food · evening · Year-round

Photography

Ride a hot air balloon over the Angkor temple complex

A tethered balloon near Angkor Wat offers a wide aerial view over the temple grounds and surrounding jungle canopy.

culture · morning · Year-round, best Nov–Feb · $37

Beaches

Relax on the beaches of Koh Rong island

A less-developed island escape off the coast near Sihanoukville, with clear water, hammocks, and bioluminescent plankton visible some nights.

Common questions

Do I need a visa for Cambodia?

Most nationalities, including US, UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian citizens, can apply for an e-visa online before arrival, valid for a 30-day stay; visa on arrival is also available at major airports and land borders.

How many days does Angkor need?

A 3-day pass is the standard choice and lets you see Angkor Wat, Bayon, and Ta Prohm without rushing, plus time for lesser-visited outer temples that see far fewer crowds.

Should I visit the Khmer Rouge historical sites?

Many travelers find Tuol Sleng and the Killing Fields near Phnom Penh essential for understanding modern Cambodia, though they're emotionally heavy, pace your itinerary to allow processing time afterward.

Is Cambodia affordable?

Yes, among the more budget-friendly destinations in Southeast Asia, with US dollars used interchangeably for most transactions, simplifying budgeting for American travelers specifically.

Siem Reap or Phnom Penh, which as a base?

Siem Reap is the gateway to Angkor and the more tourist-oriented of the two; Phnom Penh offers deeper insight into Cambodia's modern history and a livelier riverside dining scene.

How much does an Angkor Wat pass cost?

A one-day Angkor Pass costs $37, a three-day pass (usable over a week) costs $62, and a seven-day pass costs $72: all covering Angkor Wat plus every other temple in the archaeological park, including outlying sites like Banteay Srei. Buying online at least a day ahead saves 30-45 minutes in the entry queue, and children under 12 get in free with a passport.
