Things to do in Equatorial Guinea

Africa's only Spanish-speaking country, an island capital built almost entirely from oil wealth in the space of a generation, and rainforest holding some of the least-disturbed gorilla habitat left on the continent.

Travel smart

Bottled or purified water only. Not expected; small tips are appreciated for service.

Equatorial Guinea is the only country in Africa where Spanish is an official language, a legacy of Spanish colonial rule that makes it a genuinely unusual linguistic outlier on the continent, alongside Portuguese and French as co-official languages. Malabo, its capital on Bioko Island, has been dramatically rebuilt over the past few decades using the country's substantial oil wealth, creating a striking, purpose-built modern skyline that stands in sharp contrast to the surrounding rainforest and undeveloped mainland region of Río Muni.

When to go

Dry season (Bioko Island)December–February

The most comfortable window on Bioko Island, with lower rainfall for exploring Malabo and the island's interior.

Wet season (Bioko Island)March–November

Regular and often heavy rain on Bioko, one of the wettest places in Africa, though the rainforest is at its most lush.

Dry season (Río Muni mainland)June–September

A different, drier window on the mainland region, offset from Bioko Island's seasonal pattern.

Wet season (Río Muni mainland)October–May

Heavier rain on the mainland, following a distinct pattern from the island's wet season.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for Equatorial Guinea?

All nationalities, including US, UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian citizens, need an e-visa applied for online in advance, with a processing fee and arrival required via Malabo International Airport.

▾Why is Spanish spoken here and nowhere else in Africa?

Equatorial Guinea is the only African country where Spanish is an official language, a direct legacy of Spanish colonial rule, alongside French and Portuguese as additional co-official languages.

▾Is Equatorial Guinea expensive to visit?

Yes, among the pricier destinations in Central Africa, reflecting its oil-driven economy and currently limited, low-volume tourism infrastructure.

▾Bioko Island or Río Muni mainland — which should I prioritize?

Bioko Island holds the capital, Malabo, and more developed infrastructure; the Río Muni mainland offers denser, less-visited rainforest and closer access to Gabon's Central African wildlife corridors.

▾How many days does Equatorial Guinea need?

7-9 days lets you combine Malabo's modern development with Bioko Island's rainforest interior, given the country's currently limited tourism infrastructure and travel logistics to the mainland.

▾How much does it cost to visit Monte Alen National Park?

Foreign visitor entry runs roughly $30-50 USD, typically requiring a guide arranged in advance given the park's limited infrastructure. Equatorial Guinea remains one of the least-visited countries in Africa for independent tourism, so most visits go through a specialist operator rather than casual planning.
