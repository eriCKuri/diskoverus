Things to do in Slovakia

A capital dwarfed by its own castle, the High Tatras' jagged alpine peaks packed into a small footprint, and medieval towns that remain some of Central Europe's least-visited gems.

Tap water is safe to drink. 5–10% is customary, often rounded to the nearest note.

Slovakia holds the world's highest concentration of castles and châteaux per capita, with several hundred scattered across a country roughly the size of West Virginia. Its High Tatras range packs genuinely dramatic, jagged alpine peaks, some over 2,600 metres, into one of the smallest mountain ranges on Earth, offering serious hiking within a short distance of the country's other main sights.

When to go

SpringApril–May

Mild and green, with the High Tatras still snow-capped and lower trails opening up as the season progresses.

SummerJune–August

Warm and the best window for High Tatras hiking, with Bratislava's outdoor café culture in full swing.

AutumnSeptember–October

Comfortable temperatures and fewer crowds, with vivid forest color in the mountains.

WinterNovember–March

Cold with reliable snow in the Tatras: an established, affordable ski destination by European standards.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for Slovakia?

US, UK, Canadian, and Australian passport holders can enter visa-free for up to 90 days within any 180-day period under Schengen rules.

▾Is Bratislava worth visiting beyond a day trip from Vienna?

Yes, though many visitors do treat it as a short add-on from Vienna: staying 2 nights lets you properly explore the castle, old town, and cross into the High Tatras or Slovak Paradise for a fuller trip.

▾How many days does Slovakia need?

6-8 days lets you combine Bratislava, a medieval town or two, and a few days of High Tatras hiking without rushing.

▾Is Slovakia easy to combine with a Central Europe trip?

Very much so: Bratislava sits within an hour of Vienna and a few hours of Budapest and Prague, making it a natural add-on to a wider regional itinerary.

▾Is Slovakia affordable?

Yes, among the more budget-friendly EU destinations, with strong value across food, accommodation, and mountain activities relative to Austria or Switzerland.

▾How much does it cost to visit Bratislava Castle?

Standard adult admission covering the castle's exhibitions runs about €10-13, while walking the castle grounds and courtyard outside is free. It's a modest cost relative to similar castle museums elsewhere in Central Europe.
