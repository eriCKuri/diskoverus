Travel smart

Bottled water recommended on smaller islands. Not customary.

Kiribati is spread across all four hemispheres and roughly 3.5 million square kilometres of ocean, an area wider than the continental United States, despite having a total land area smaller than New York City, making it one of the most geographically dispersed nations on Earth. Because of a deliberate 1995 realignment of the International Date Line to keep the whole country on the same calendar day, Kiribati's easternmost islands are the first inhabited place on the planet to greet each new day and new year.

When to go

Dry seasonMay–October

The most comfortable window, with lower rainfall and calmer seas for inter-island travel.

Wet seasonNovember–April

Warmer and more humid with regular rain, including the Pacific cyclone risk window for southern outer islands.

Peak heatMarch–May

The warmest stretch of the year, with high humidity across the low-lying atolls.

Transition periodsApril–May, October–November

Shoulder months balancing decent weather with fewer logistical complications between the two main seasons.

Common questions

▾Do I need a visa for Kiribati?

US, UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian passport holders can enter visa-free for up to 30 days.

▾How do I get to Kiribati?

International flights connect through Fiji or Nauru to Tarawa, the main atoll; onward travel to outer islands relies on infrequent domestic flights or boats, so it's worth building flexibility into any itinerary.

▾Why is Kiribati so geographically unusual?

It's spread across all four hemispheres and roughly 3.5 million square kilometres of ocean, an area wider than the continental United States, despite a total land area smaller than New York City.

▾Is Kiribati affected by climate change?

Yes, significantly: as a low-lying atoll nation, Kiribati is among the countries most exposed to sea-level rise, a reality that shapes both daily life and long-term national planning.

▾How many days does Kiribati need?

7-9 days is a reasonable window for Tarawa and a nearby outer island, though the country's extreme dispersal means most visitors focus on just one or two atolls rather than attempting to island-hop widely.

▾How much does it cost to visit Kiribati's outer islands?

There's no broad national park fee; most costs come from inter-island flights or boat transport, which can run well over $100 USD per leg given how spread out the atolls are. Kiribati remains one of the least-visited countries in the world, so infrastructure for independent tourism is minimal outside the capital, Tarawa.
